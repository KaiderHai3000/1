FAQ

What people moving to Switzerland ask us most.

As of 2026. These answers give a first overview and do not replace individual advice.

Which permit do I need as a German citizen?

Thanks to the free movement agreement between the EU and Switzerland, the process is relatively simple. With an employment contract of one year or more, or an open-ended one, you will usually receive residence permit B; shorter contracts lead to short-term permit L. If you keep living in Germany and return there at least once a week, you receive cross-border commuter permit G. After moving, register with your municipality within 14 days and before starting work.

When do I have to take out health insurance?

Within three months of arrival. Cover then applies retroactively from the day you moved. Cross-border commuters from Germany have a choice: within three months of starting work, they can opt for Swiss health insurance or for insurance in Germany. This choice can later only be changed in exceptional cases.

How much does health insurance cost in Basel?

The standard premium for adults in Basel-Stadt is CHF 694 per month in 2026 (CHF 300 deductible, including accident cover). With a higher deductible or a GP or telemedicine model, you pay noticeably less. If you are employed for at least 8 hours a week, your employer insures you against accidents, so you can exclude accident cover from your health insurance.

What happens to my German pension?

Entitlements you have built up in Germany are kept. Contribution periods in both countries count towards each other's minimum insurance periods. At retirement, each country pays the part you earned there — and your German pension can be paid into a Swiss account.

How will I be taxed in Switzerland?

With a B permit, tax is initially deducted directly from your salary (withholding tax). If your gross salary exceeds CHF 120,000 a year, an ordinary tax return follows automatically. Below that, you can request one by 31 March of the following year, for example to deduct pillar 3a contributions. Cross-border commuters pay at most 4.5% withholding tax in Switzerland and are taxed in Germany, where the Swiss tax is credited.

How much can I pay into pillar 3a?

Employees with a pension fund can pay in up to CHF 7,258 in 2026. Without a pension fund, the limit is 20% of earned income, up to CHF 36,288. Contributions are deductible from taxable income.
